# Osaka keep: roof revision

**Audience:** Contributors and coding agents. Historical research and asset notes, not a user guide; details may no longer match the current app.

Visual references inspected for the revision:

- [Osaka Castle Museum exterior photo gallery](https://www.osakacastle.net/photo_shinsei/gaikan/), especially samples 001 and 006 for the exterior silhouette.
- [Osaka Junior Chamber architectural walk](https://www.osaka-jc.or.jp/activities/2017/12/01/468/): [upper facade close-up](https://www.osaka-jc.or.jp/2025/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/thum_daiosaka06-1.jpg).
- [Cycling and Exploring Osaka](https://www.bikeridingtales.com/blog/2019/2/cycling-osaka-6jchp): upper-facade photograph showing the gables, gold trim, black upper story, and balcony.

The first example relied on shallow concentric roof bands, regular setbacks, and small face ornaments. The close-ups instead show substantial pitched roof areas, dominant gables, an enclosed dark upper story with a balcony, and a pronounced top roof. These visual observations guided the revision; dimensions are artistic estimates, not measured reconstruction data.

The examples now use separate half swept roof panels for every main roof plane. Four tapered panels form a skirt with matching hip seams; paired rectangular halves form projecting gable roofs and the crown. The high-edge width is adjustable so a panel can be rectangular or trapezoidal. Gable faces match the same curved profile rather than merely placing a triangular ornament on a shallow ring. Large front/back gables and smaller side gables alternate, the upper story is broader, and the roof curve is less exaggerated. Existing complete roof parts remain available for other buildings.

The simplified keep remains an approximation: reliefs, joinery, rear elevations, internal floors, exact tier spacing, and historic fabric are not reproduced faithfully. Reference photographs were viewed for design guidance and are not bundled as assets.
